# Council Proposal 1 - A proposal to replace all the office PC's with a hot desk solution.

## Explanation of this proposal
*What do you think this proposal might mean? How might it be implemented?*

A study of employees in the finance industry tested the preposition that work team indentity is more salient than organizational identity when desks are assigned, whereas organizational identity is more salient when they are not; and that this is partly because physical arrangements have a significant bearing on the way in which employees engage withthe organization as well as who they are most likely to engage with (i.e., impacting on the type and focus of organizationalparticipation). 

The study measured levels of work team and organizational identity in matched samples of employees(N=142) assigned to desks and not assigned (i.e., hot desked), as well as their perceptions of the use, importance, andeffectiveness of electronic and face-to-face communication as indicators of different types of organizational participation.Results support the hypotheses. The perceived value of electronic communication also accounted for signiﬁcant variancein organizational identiﬁcation for all employees. Findings point to a number of practical implications relating to the useof hot desking in the workplace. 

The organizational attachments formed by employ-ees are of increasing interest within the contempo-rary workplace where, in recent years, the prospect ofsecure organizational membership has become increas-ingly remote (Rousseau 2001, Veenstra et al. 2004).One issue that has become particularly topical relates tofears that economic imperatives have created ﬂexible butvacuous organizational entities that dislocate employ-ees physically (through spatial and temporal decoupling)and psychologically from the workplace, fundamentallychanging the way they engage with the organization(e.g., Baruch 1998, Handy 1994). Examples include anincrease in the use of teleworkers (where employeeswork from home or from a portable ofﬁce-e.g., in acar) and hot desking (where employees are not assigneddedicated desks but work from any that happen to bevacant).

This study provides strong support for the propositionthat physical factors (e.g., colocation, interpersonal con-tact and interaction) are not necessary for identiﬁcation-based attachments to be formed, but that the physicalconditions of a job may nonetheless have implicationsfor both the focus and the mechanisms of attachment.Regarding the focus of identiﬁcation, it was found thatdesk assignment advantaged team identity over orga-nizational identity, with the opposite pattern emergingamong employees without assigned desks. Regarding the mechanisms of attachment, we found that differ-ent desk arrangements systematically changed the pri-mary medium of active participation in organizationallife, with implications for the dynamics of psychologicalsalience. In particular, desk assignment accentuatedthe perceived value of face-to-face communication and,through this, was a powerful predictor of team identity,while nonassignment of desks accentuated the per-ceived value of electronic communication and, throughthis, was a powerful predictor of organizational identity.We believe that the links we have exposed herebetween contextual factors, active participation, and thedynamics of psychological salience provide importantinsights into the dynamics of teamwork and organizationlife. Importantly, these make the case for taking the anal-ysis of organizational space into a psychological realmbeyond purely physical principles of ergonomics, archi-tecture, and space management. This can also providewhat Dunbar and Starbuck (2006, p. 173) call a bridge“between organizational knowledge that is based onacademic research and organizational design contribu-tions that are considered useful based on organizationalneeds.” Space may not be the ﬁnal frontier for organi-zational psychologists, but we should now be motivatedto go there more boldly.


[Millward, L.J., Haslam, S.A. and Postmes, T., 2007. Putting employees in their place: The impact of hot desking on organizational and team identification. Organization Science, 18(4), pp.547-559.](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/S-Haslam/publication/258278295_2007_OS_Space/links/0deec527acf3b90223000000/2007-OS-Space.pdf)
