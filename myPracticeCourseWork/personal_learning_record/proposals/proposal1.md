# Council Proposal 1 - A proposal to replace all the office PC's with a hot desk solution.

## Explanation of this proposal
*What do you think this proposal might mean? How might it be implemented?*

A study of employees in the finance industry tested the preposition that work team indentity is more salient than organizational identity when desks are assigned, whereas organizational identity is more salient when they are not; and that this is partly because physical arrangements have a significant bearing on the way in which employees engage withthe organization as well as who they are most likely to engage with (i.e., impacting on the type and focus of organizationalparticipation). 

The study measured levels of work team and organizational identity in matched samples of employees(N=142) assigned to desks and not assigned (i.e., hot desked), as well as their perceptions of the use, importance, andeffectiveness of electronic and face-to-face communication as indicators of different types of organizational participation.Results support the hypotheses. The perceived value of electronic communication also accounted for signiﬁcant variancein organizational identiﬁcation for all employees. Findings point to a number of practical implications relating to the useof hot desking in the workplace. 

The organizational attachments formed by employees are of increasing interest within the contemporary workplace where, in recent years, the prospect ofsecure organizational membership has become increasingly remote (Rousseau 2001, Veenstra et al. 2004).One issue that has become particularly topical relates tofears that economic imperatives have created ﬂexible butvacuous organizational entities that dislocate employees physically (through spatial and temporal decoupling) and psychologically from the workplace, fundamentally changing the way they engage with the organization (e.g., Baruch 1998, Handy 1994). Examples include an increase in the use of teleworkers (where employeeswork from home or from a portable ofﬁce-e.g., in acar) and hot desking (where employees are not assigned dedicated desks but work from any that happen to be vacant).

This study provides strong support for the propositionthat physical factors (e.g., colocation, interpersonal con-tact and interaction) are not necessary for identiﬁcation-based attachments to be formed, but that the physicalconditions of a job may nonetheless have implicationsfor both the focus and the mechanisms of attachment. Regarding the focus of identiﬁcation, it was found that desk assignment advantaged team identity over organizational identity, with the opposite pattern emergingamong employees without assigned desks. Regarding the mechanisms of attachment, we found that different desk arrangements systematically changed the primary medium of active participation in organizational life, with implications for the dynamics of psychologicalsalience. In particular, desk assignment accentuatedthe perceived value of face-to-face communication and, through this, was a powerful predictor of team identity,while non-assignment of desks accentuated the perceived value of electronic communication and, through this, was a powerful predictor of organizational identity. We believe that the links we have exposed herebetween contextual factors, active participation, and the dynamics of psychological salience provide important insights into the dynamics of teamwork and organization life. Importantly, these make the case for taking the analysis of organizational space into a psychological realm beyond purely physical principles of ergonomics, architecture, and space management. This can also provide what Dunbar and Starbuck (2006, p. 173) call a bridge “between organizational knowledge that is based on academic research and organizational design contributions that are considered useful based on organizational needs.” Space may not be the ﬁnal frontier for organizational psychologists, but we should now be motivated to go there more boldly.


[Millward, L.J., Haslam, S.A. and Postmes, T., 2007. Putting employees in their place: The impact of hot desking on organizational and team identification. Organization Science, 18(4), pp.547-559.](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/S-Haslam/publication/258278295_2007_OS_Space/links/0deec527acf3b90223000000/2007-OS-Space.pdf)
