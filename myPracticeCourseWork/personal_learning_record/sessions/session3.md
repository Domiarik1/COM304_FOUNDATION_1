Experiments with IO on my Raspberry Pi: Almost all publications cite low cost of hardware, ease of availability and the advantages of a substantial community as the reasons for basing their work on the Raspberry Pi. We aim to represent a variety of use cases and area disciplines that utilise the Raspberry Pi but it is by no means exhaustive. The predominant ussage of the Raspberry Pi is, rather unsuprisingly, for educational purposes. This includes both hardware and software, in a range of educational and research facilities, many of the other publications included in the special issue address specific trends. For example, with the availability of low-cost compututing, we are seeing a change in architectures, whereby computing is pushed towards the edge of the network, This Fog or Edge computing is an important change that is required to make IoT system more efficient and scalable. Scaling to billions of devices will only be possible if power is used efficiently through optimised computing and intelligent monitoring systems. This will have an impact on the environment in which we live. Understanding climate change, pollution and other environmental issues can benefit from IoT devices that measures and log parameters. With the creation of huge numbers of IoT devices, alternative networking models, strategies and mechanisms are required; one tool in this area of research is network testbeds. These testbeds help bridge the worlds of pure simulation with experimental design. Physical testbeds can be costly, making Raspberry Pi-based solution more attractive.
